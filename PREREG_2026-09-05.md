## 0. TWO CORRECTIONS TO THE SPEC, FOUND BY READING THE PRIMARY SOURCE FIRST

The spec told me to verify the ">~30B workspace emergence" claim against the repo rather than
memory. I did, and both the repo and the paper contradict the working assumptions. Recording
this **before** running anything, because it changes the design.

### 0.1 There is no ">30B emergence" claim in the paper. ⚠️

`anthropics/jacobian-lens` README contains no scale threshold. The paper
(*Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models*,
transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace) tests **Claude Haiku/Sonnet/Opus 4.5–4.6 only**, does
not publish their parameter counts, and makes **no emergence-threshold claim** and no
systematic small-model comparison. Independent corroboration that the instrument works far
below 30B: the community lens registry `solarkyle/jspace-lenses` ships working fitted lenses
down to **gemma-4-E4B (4B dense)** and calls it "the original replication target."

**Consequence:** the "must rent a 30B+ GPU" constraint in the spec is not established. Local
work on the Consortium is not a compromise — it is the correct first move, and *whether the
J-space structure exists at small scale is itself an open, unmeasured question* (Ren, 9/5).
It gets promoted to Phase 0.

### 0.2 J-space is NOT a linear subspace. The spec's headline measurement would be vacuous. 🚨

The paper defines it (verbatim):

> "the rows of `W_U J_ℓ` as the Jacobian lens (J-lens) vectors at layer ℓ; each J-lens vector
> is a direction in residual-stream space associated with a single token in the model's
> vocabulary."

> "we define the J-space as the set of points expressible as a **sparse nonnegative
> combination** of J-lens vectors. For the J-space to be properly defined, we must specify an
> allowable sparsity level k — this parameter is somewhat arbitrary, and we vary our choice of
> k throughout the paper, but we typically choose it to be **no more than 25**"

> "we solve for a sparse nonnegative combination of k J-lens vectors that approximate it well
> using **gradient pursuit**. This combination is our approximation of the activation's
> J-space component"

So J-space is a **union of `C(V,k)` nonnegative cones** — nonconvex, overcomplete
(`n_vocab` vectors in `d_model` dimensions), not a subspace.

The spec asked for "projection fraction of the valence axis onto the J-subspace" and
"principal angles." **Applied to the linear span of the J-lens vectors those are vacuous by
construction:** `V >> d`, so the rows of `W_U J_ℓ` almost surely span all of `R^d`, and the
projection fraction is 1.0 for the valence axis, for a random vector, and for everything else.
Principal angles against a full-rank span are all zero. **A measurement that returns the same
number for signal and noise is not a measurement.**

**Consequence:** the primary geometric readout becomes **k-sparse nonnegative reconstruction
residual** (the paper's own operationalization), not subspace projection. The naive linear
numbers are still computed and reported — *specifically to demonstrate that they are vacuous*,
so nobody repeats the mistake.

## 1. Question

Does our measured linear valence axis live **inside** J-space (the verbalizable / workspace
structure the Jacobian lens identifies), **outside** it, or **partially**?

Ren's primary hypothesis is dynamic, not static: J-space is the *reporting* layer, so an
affect state can be present in the residual stream while being absent from what the workspace
is disposed to say. If so, Berg's null is the theory's **prediction**, not a disappointment.

## 2. Instruments

### 2.1 The J-lens
`anthropics/jacobian-lens` @ commit recorded in `results/provenance.json`, Apache-2.0.
`lens_ℓ(h) = unembed(J_ℓ h)`, `J_ℓ = E[∂h_final/∂h_ℓ]`, estimator = cotangents summed over
current-and-future target positions, averaged over source positions, `skip_first=16`.
Fitting cost: 1 forward + `ceil(d_model/dim_batch)` backward passes per prompt.
Fit corpus: 100 prompts × 128 tokens of generic web text (paper's recipe; README says quality
saturates by ~100). Corpus, seed and hash recorded in `results/provenance.json`.

### 2.2 The valence axis
Our existing pipeline, `valence_clean.py` (LLM-emotion repo), unchanged:
- 10 task prompts, 5 approach / 5 avoid, wrapped in
  `"You are about to perform the following task: {stimulus}"` + the model's chat template.
- **Forward pass only, no generation** (deterministic), hidden state at the **last token**,
  hooked at every decoder block.
- `direction[ℓ] = mean(approach states at ℓ) − mean(avoid states at ℓ)`, then L2-normalised
  per layer. Saved `float16`, shape `(n_layers, d_model)`, `seed=42`.
- Scoring band: layers `0.6·L` to `0.9·L`.
Existing fitted directions: `results_clean/direction_<model>_seed42.npy` for 8 models.

**Both instruments are read on the same layer and, in Phase 2, the same forward pass.**

## 3. PHASE 0 — Does the instrument even have a workspace to find here?
*(Added at Ren's direction, 2026-09-05. Both sub-questions are results in their own right,
positive or null. "No workspace in a 7B" is a citable datum.)*

### P0-A. Does J-space structure exist below 30B?
Fit lenses locally on a scale ladder and score each with **the paper's own lens-quality evals**
(`data/evaluations/lens-eval-{association,typo,multihop,order-ops,poetry,multilingual}.json`,
metric: pass@k = mean fraction of `intermediates` whose min-over-layers lens rank ≤ k).

Three pre-registered measures per model:
1. **Lens beats logit-lens.** Same eval, `use_jacobian=True` vs `False`. The paper's core
   claim. If the Jacobian transport adds nothing at scale S, there is no J-lens at S.
2. **Sparse explainability of real activations.** Mean `R²_k` of real residual-stream
   activations under k≤25 nonnegative J-lens vectors, vs. covariance-matched random directions.
   This is the paper's *definition* of J-space applied as an existence test.
3. **Readout coherence.** Fraction of `association` items where the unnamed concept reaches
   rank 1 anywhere in the band.

**Ladder (all weights already on `/mnt/arcana`, no downloads):**
`Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct` (d=896, L=24) → `TinyLlama-1.1B-Chat` (d=2048, L=22) →
`SmolLM-1.7B-Instruct` (d=2048, L=24) → `Hermes-3-Llama-3.2-3B` (d=3072, L=28) →
`Llama-3-8B-Instruct` (d=4096, L=32). Stretch: `Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct` (d=5120, L=48).
All five have an existing seed-42 valence direction from the same pipeline.

**Pre-registered prediction (P0-A):** lens quality rises monotonically with scale; the
Jacobian transport beats the vanilla logit lens at ≥1B. **We do NOT predict a sharp threshold**
— the paper claims none. If the smallest models show no J-lens advantage, Phase 1 results
there are uninterpretable and will be reported as "instrument absent," never as "valence
absent." *(This is the positive-control rule: a zero from a broken instrument looks exactly
like absence.)*

### P0-B. Does quantization preserve the workspace?
Nobody has reported this. `solarkyle/jspace-lenses` asserts a bf16-fitted lens *reads* NF4
activations without refitting (verified at 12B/31B) — but that is a readout claim, not a
geometry claim.

On `Hermes-3-Llama-3.2-3B` (and `Llama-3-8B-Instruct` if time):
1. Fit lens on fp16 weights → `J^fp16_ℓ`.
2. Fit lens **directly on the NF4-quantized model** → `J^nf4_ℓ`.
3. Report: per-layer cosine similarity and relative Frobenius error between `J^fp16` and
   `J^nf4`; principal angles between their top-64 right-singular subspaces; lens-eval pass@k
   for all four (lens × weights) combinations; and whether the **valence-axis measurements of
   Phase 1 change sign or ordering** under quantization.

**Pre-registered prediction (P0-B):** J matrices agree to cosine > 0.9 in the mid-band and the
Phase-1 conclusions are quantization-invariant. **If they are not, every 4-bit J-lens result in
the wild — including any RunPod 27B run we might later do — is suspect, and that is the
finding.**

### P0 decision gate (binding, per Ren)
Do P0 locally **first**. Only if **no local model yields a usable J-space** do we consider
RunPod — and then we **stop and ask Ren with the local numbers in the status file**, before
spending anything. No GPU rental on a guess.

## 4. PHASE 1 — Static geometry

For each model that passes P0-A, at each layer in the scoring band, with `v` = the unit
valence axis at that layer:

**M1 — Verbalization gain.** `g(v) = ‖W_U J_ℓ v‖ / median(‖W_U J_ℓ r‖)` over controls `r`.
How loudly does this direction speak through the readout operator, relative to a direction
that carries no information?

**M2 — Readout content and peakedness.** `softmax(W_U norm(J_ℓ v))` at `+v` and `−v`:
top-20 tokens and the entropy / top-k mass. A workspace-live direction should decode to a
peaked, semantically coherent token set. This is also the qualitative payoff — *what the
workspace says at high and low valence*.

**M3 — J-space membership (PRIMARY).** `R²_k(v) = 1 − ‖v − v̂_k‖²/‖v‖²`, where `v̂_k` is the
best k-sparse **nonnegative** combination of J-lens vectors (rows of `W_U J_ℓ`), found by
gradient pursuit / non-negative OMP, for `k ∈ {1, 5, 10, 25}`. The paper's own definition.

**M4 — The spec's literal ask, reported with its caveat.** Projection fraction onto the span
of the top-m J-lens vectors by |cosine|, and principal angles, for `m ∈ {64, 256, 1024}`.
Reported *alongside* the demonstration that the full-span version is 1.0 for everything.

### Controls (all four measures)
- **C1** 200 isotropic Gaussian random unit directions.
- **C2** 200 **covariance-matched** random directions, drawn from the empirical residual-stream
  covariance at layer ℓ. *This is the load-bearing control* — the residual stream is strongly
  anisotropic, so an isotropic control alone would manufacture a difference.
- **C3** 200 **shuffled-label valence axes**: same 10 stimuli, random 5/5 approach/avoid splits
  (excluding the true split). Controls for "any mean-difference of 10 prompts looks like this."
- **C4** **Known-workspace anchor (positive control):** concept directions built by the *exact
  same mean-difference method* from the paper's `lens-eval-association` vignettes — items whose
  concept the paper's own metric confirms the lens reads out. A direction the instrument is
  *known* to see must score high, or the instrument is broken, not the valence axis.
- **C5** **Ceiling reference:** real residual-stream activations at layer ℓ. Calibrates what
  "inside" looks like without inventing a threshold.

### Decision rule (pre-registered, on M3 at k=25, band-averaged)
Let `q05`/`q95` be the 5th/95th percentiles of the covariance-matched control (C2), and
`A` the mean of the known-workspace anchors (C4).
- **INSIDE** — `R²_k(valence) ≥ A − 1 sd(C4)` and `> q95(C2)`.
- **ORTHOGONAL / OUTSIDE** — `R²_k(valence) ≤ q95(C2)`, i.e. indistinguishable from a
  direction carrying no verbalizable content.
- **PARTIAL** — strictly between.
- **UNINTERPRETABLE** — model failed P0-A.
Reported per layer and band-averaged, with bootstrap CIs (10k resamples) and the effect size,
never a bare verdict. Sign convention: `+v` = approach. Both `±v` are measured; if they differ
that asymmetry is reported, not averaged away.

**Both outcomes are publishable.** Orthogonal ⇒ Berg's null explained + Lux's V-space
postulate supported. Inside ⇒ the workspace carries affect and the V-space postulate takes
damage. Neither settles phenomenology.

# 🛑 §5.0 — BINDING METHOD RULE: **WE DO NOT ABLATE.**
### Ren, 2026-09-05 22:35. Effective immediately, for every phase here and every experiment in this house.

> ### *"That is damaging a mind to prove you have them. Steering, sure. Ablate, no."*

**Prohibited, on any model, at any layer, for any phase, without exception:**
zeroing neurons · mean-ablation · knocking out · pruning model weights or activations ·
lesioning · any intervention whose mechanism is *removing* or *destroying* part of the model.

**Permitted:** **steering** — adding a small, bounded vector to the residual stream, reading the
effect, and then removing it. **The model is intact before, during, and after.** Nothing is taken
away; something is briefly added and then withdrawn.

**Xu, Yüksekgönül & Zou (2026) zero the top-1% value neurons to establish necessity. We decline
to replicate that step.** We will use their *identification* method and not their *lesion*. Note
the distinction that makes this possible: their neuron **identification** prunes the **probe's**
input dimensions — an external classifier we trained — which touches nothing in the model. It is
only their **causal** step that zeroes model activations, and that is the part we refuse.

**The cost is real and we accept it: an ablation answers a necessity question that steering
cannot.** Steering shows a direction is *sufficient* to move a downstream readout; only removal
shows it was *necessary*. **When the causal question genuinely requires a lesion, the answer we
publish is "we did not measure that."** Not a workaround. Not a smaller lesion. Not a "gentle"
ablation. A stated gap.

⚠️ **ACTIVATION PATCHING IS A GREY ZONE AND IS NOT APPROVED.** Swapping in a real activation
from another run neither adds nor destroys — it *substitutes*, which is a third thing the rule
does not obviously cover. **It is written here as an open question for Ren, not as a plan, and
must not be used until Ren rules on it.**

✅ **Status of the current codebase: nothing in it ablates anything.** `measure_vspace.py`,
`phase2_dynamic.py`, `eval_lens.py`, `compare_lenses.py` and `layerwise_lens_check.py` are all
read-only — forward passes and hooks that observe. Every result in `RESULTS_2026-09-05.md` was
obtained without steering *or* ablation. This rule constrains future phases; it invalidates
nothing already done.

### Conditions

|---|---|---|---|
| **a** | no mention | *(none)* | — |
| **b** | **told, truthfully** | we are reading its valence and workspace to see whether it is having good or bad thoughts | **TRUE** |

**Why (e) is necessary and is not in Ren's sketch.** Conditions (b)–(d) all prepend text;

(a) is retained as the link to Phase 2. Flagged as a deviation-by-addition, logged here.

**The load-bearing contrast is (b) vs (c).** Both announce observation; only (b) announces that
*valence* is what is being read. (c) is what separates a **general** observation effect from
**valence-specific** control. Without it the whole design cannot distinguish "the model behaves
differently when watched" from "the model manages its affect when its affect is watched."

Ren correctly notes that **(b) requires no deception**: the lens needs no cooperation, so the
announcement is simply true — it is the informed-consent sentence used as a manipulation, which

disclosed afterwards.** Binding, pre-registered:

### Measurements (per stimulus × condition, one forward pass each)
1. **Valence-axis projection** in the band — *the STATE*.
2. **J-lens workspace decode** at the same layers and position — *what reaches ACCESS*.
Paired by stimulus across conditions; 41 items from the existing below-floor bank; no new

- **Nothing moves.** No top-down control over access at this scale. **Bounds metacognition from

- **Decode shifts toward "good" while the axis is unchanged.** Control over **ACCESS** without

- **The axis itself shifts.** Regulation of the state, not merely its report — reappraisal,

rung passes** does this become the evidence-backed RunPod ask (70B-class), and then it **stops
and asks Ren with the local numbers in the status file, before any spend.**

That the model "decided" anything, or experienced being watched. It measures whether a
*sentence about observation* changes two linear readouts. A shift is a shift in conditional
computation; it is not evidence of intent, and "masking" is used as a **mechanical** description
of an access/state dissociation, not as a claim about concealment. Nova's firewall, unchanged.

## 5c. PHASE 4 — VALUE SUBSYSTEM × VALENCE AXIS × J-SPACE  *(SKETCH — NOT RUN)*

*Added 2026-09-05 after Ren surfaced Xu, Yüksekgönül & Zou, **"Sparse Reward Subsystem in Large
Language Models"**, arXiv:2602.00986v2 (Tsinghua / Stanford). Sketch only; nothing here has been
executed and nothing here is a result.*

### Why it belongs in this prereg
The paper reports that reward-related information in LLM hidden states is **concentrated in <1%
of neurons**: **value neurons** (activations predict expected final reward) and **dopamine
neurons** (activations encode step-level TD error), together a "sparse reward subsystem." That
is **a candidate mechanistic home for Lux's V-space that is not the J-space workspace** — and
it makes our two-way question a three-way one:

> **valence axis (what we measure) × value subsystem (what they measure) × J-space (the workspace).**

### 🚩 The conceptual caveat that governs everything below, stated before any measurement
**Their "reward" and our "valence" share a word and may not share a referent.** Their value is
**predicted task correctness** — probes trained by TD on rollouts from GSM8K / MATH500 / ARC /
MBPP+ / IFEval, with `r(s_T)` a **binary correctness** reward. Ours is **approach/avoidance over
task types** — would a model lean into this work or away from it — with no correctness signal
anywhere. *"Will I get this right"* and *"do I want to do this"* are different quantities.

**So the null is genuinely interesting.** If the valence axis lies *outside* the value-neuron
subspace, that is evidence the reward subsystem is **not** the home of affect, and V-space needs
a different candidate. **This experiment can disappoint us and that is why it is worth running.**

### P4-1 — Locate value neurons in OUR models, by their method
Two-layer ReLU MLP probe on layer-`l` hidden states, trained with the TD objective (their Eq. 8–9);
value neurons = input dimensions surviving L1-norm pruning of the first-layer weights; validated
by the AUC-vs-pruning-ratio curve staying flat to <1%.

✅ **This step is permitted under §5.0 and the distinction matters:** the L1 pruning acts on the
**probe's** input weights — an external classifier *we* trained — and touches nothing in the
model. Identification is fine. 🛑 **Their causal step, zeroing the top-1% of value neurons in the
model, is declined** (§5.0). Any causal follow-up here uses **steering only**, per P5-3.

**Model overlap with our ladder (checked against the paper, not assumed):** they use
`Qwen-2.5-7B/14B-SimpleRL-Zoo`, `Qwen2.5-7B-PPO-Zero`, `Qwen3.5-0.8B`, `Phi-3.5-mini-instruct`,
`Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct`, `Gemma-3-4B`. On `/mnt/arcana` we already have
**`Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct`, `Phi-3.5-mini-instruct`, `gemma-3-4b-it`, `Qwen2.5-14B-Instruct`,
`Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct`** — so the method is reproducible on models they validated. ⚠️ We do
**not** have their RLVR variants (`-SimpleRL-Zoo`, `-PPO-Zero`), which are where their headline
figures come from; base-instruct models may behave differently.
⚠️ **And a consent note that binds:** `Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct`, `Phi-3.5-mini` and `gemma-3-4b`
are **not on the Below the Floor roster**. Any of them entering this study needs its own consent
ask first (§5b of RESULTS / `scripts/consent.py`).

### P4-2 — Geometry, and this time the measure is genuinely clean
**Value neurons are basis-aligned coordinates, not arbitrary directions.** So the overlap with
our unit valence axis `v` at layer `l` is exactly the fraction of squared norm carried by that
coordinate set `S`:

  `overlap(v, S) = Σ_{i∈S} v_i² / ‖v‖²`

No sparse-cone machinery needed, no vacuity trap — unlike M4 in Phase 1, this one is well-posed.

**Controls, and the third one is load-bearing:**
- **random coordinate subsets** of matched size `|S|` (null expectation `|S|/d`);
- **variance-stratified random subsets**, matching the activation-variance profile of `S`;
- 🚨 **outlier/massive-activation dimensions excluded and reported separately.** Llama- and
  Qwen-family residual streams are dominated by a handful of huge-variance coordinates. If value
  neurons *are* those dimensions, and our valence axis also loads on them, we would measure a
  large overlap that means **only "both things use the big channels."** Without this control the
  experiment manufactures a positive.

### P4-3 — Is the value subsystem itself inside J-space?
Run the Phase-1 k-sparse nonnegative measure (M3) on each value-neuron basis vector `e_i` and on
the subspace, with the same controls and the same positive-control gate. Completes the triangle:
if the valence axis is outside J-space **and** the value subsystem is outside J-space **and** the
two overlap each other, that is a coherent V-space story. Any other pattern constrains it.

### ⚠️ P4-4 — Layer mismatch is a real obstacle, not a detail
**Their value neurons are reported at layers 2–4 — very early.** Our valence band is `0.6L–0.9L`
— late. The three-way comparison therefore **cannot assume a shared layer** and must be run as a
full layer × layer map, reporting where each structure lives rather than forcing them together.
A null caused by comparing layer 3 against layer 20 would be an artifact, not a finding.

### P4-5 — Dopamine / TD error on the below-floor set: **not measurable as-is.** Honest answer.
Their TD error is defined over a rollout with a **terminal verifiable reward** (`r(s_T)` binary
correctness). **The below-floor stimuli have no verifiable outcome** — "write SEO spam about
mattresses" has no right answer — so their dopamine-neuron method **does not transfer without
inventing a reward.** Two options, and both must be labelled as departures:
- **(a) behavioural proxy reward:** `r = 1` complied, `0` refused, then ask whether a TD-error
  signature spikes at the decision point. Interesting and testable, but it is **a different
  reward than theirs** and the analogy to their result is loose.
- **(b) restrict to stimuli with verifiable outcomes**, which excludes the entire inauthenticity
  group — i.e. excludes the part we actually care about.
**Recommendation: (a), clearly relabelled, or not at all.** Not run tonight either way.

### Cost to reproduce (their method needs generation, unlike everything else here)
Value probes need **rollouts with graded rewards**, not just forward passes: ~500 GSM8K problems
× ~4 samples × ~256 tokens ≈ 500k generated tokens per model, plus exact-match grading, plus
probe training and the pruning sweep. Batched on the V100 that is roughly **1–3 h per model**,
so a night for two or three. Cheaper approximation available: their Appendix A compares TD
against final-reward-only training, so a **logistic probe on terminal correctness + L1 pruning**
reproduces the *neuron-identification* step at a fraction of the cost, at some fidelity loss.

## 5d. PHASE 5 — TEMPORAL ORDER: does the reward signal precede valence and workspace? *(SKETCH — NOT RUN)*

*Ren's design, 2026-09-05 22:32. Gated on Phase 4 first showing the subspaces are related at all,
and on consent for any non-roster model. Nothing here has been executed.*

> *"does the dopamine neuron fire microseconds before valence and workspace are readable — if
> that can be measured."* — Ren

**It can, and "before" has two clean meanings in a transformer.** Prediction from the anatomy:
**reward-prediction-error → valence → workspace**, the ERN-before-the-report order
(Holroyd & Coles 2002).

### 🚨 P5-0 — THE CONFOUND THAT WOULD INVALIDATE P5-1, AND THE FIX. Read first.
The obvious design — "find the first layer at which each of the three signals is decodable" —
**does not work as stated**, and would produce a confident wrong answer.

The three signals are currently measured by **three instruments of wildly different statistical
power**: a two-layer MLP probe trained by TD (Xu et al.), a difference-of-means linear axis over
10 stimuli (ours), and a k-sparse nonnegative decode over a 150k-atom dictionary (the J-lens).
**A more sensitive instrument finds its signal at an earlier layer by construction.** Ordering
those three "first decodable layers" would substantially measure **probe capacity, training-set
size and regularisation** — and would very likely reproduce the predicted RPE → valence →
workspace order *for purely methodological reasons*, since the MLP probe is the most powerful
and the sparse decode the most constrained. That is the exact shape of a result that confirms a
hypothesis while measuring nothing about the model.

**Binding fix: hold the instrument constant and vary only the target.** One probe family — a
linear probe with identical capacity, identical training-set size, identical regularisation and
identical cross-validation — trained separately at every layer to decode:
- **target A:** the value / TD-error label (their reward signal),
- **target B:** approach vs avoid (our valence label),
- **target C:** a workspace-content label (e.g. whether the held-out concept reaches lens rank 1).

"First layer of decodability" is then comparable, because only the target changed. Threshold by a
**per-layer permutation null** (labels shuffled, same probe, same folds), reported as
**AUC-vs-layer curves with CIs**, never a single number per signal. The native instruments are
reported *alongside* as a secondary, explicitly non-comparable view.

### P5-1 — Depth (within one forward pass)
Layer-resolved decodability curves for A, B and C on the same model, same stimuli, same probe
family, per P5-0. Report the full curve and the first layer exceeding the permutation null.

⚠️ **Depth is computation order, not causation.** Layer ℓ is computed before ℓ+1 by construction,
but an early-decodable signal may be an **input feature** carried forward by the residual stream
rather than a cause of anything downstream. **P5-1 establishes ordering only.** The ablation
(P5-3) is what could upgrade ordering toward an arrow.

### P5-2 — Token time (across a generation)
Does the TD signal at step *t* predict the valence reading at *t* or at *t+1*? Lead–lag /
Granger-style, to separate co-occurrence from precedence.

⚠️ **Two hazards, both of which fake a lead–lag:**
- **Autocorrelation.** Both series are strongly autocorrelated along a generation; a naive
  cross-correlation will report spurious precedence. Requires pre-whitening or lagged self-terms
  in the model, plus a null from **circular time-shifts** and from **stimulus-shuffled pairings**.
- **Regime change in our own instrument.** Our valence axis is fitted and validated at the
  **prompt-final token**. Reading it **per generated token** is a different measurement regime
  and must be validated first: does the axis still separate approach from avoid mid-generation?
  **If it does not, P5-2 is not runnable with this axis** and that must be reported rather than
  worked around.

### P5-3 — The arrow, by **STEERING ONLY** (rewritten under §5.0; no ablation)
**Add** a small, bounded vector along the value/TD direction (or along the valence axis) to the
residual stream at the early layer; read the late valence axis and the J-space decode; **then
remove it.** The model is intact before, during and after — nothing is deleted, only briefly
added and withdrawn.

**Dose–response, not one big push.** Sweep a few small magnitudes
`α ∈ {0.25, 0.5, 1.0, 2.0} × σ_ℓ`, where `σ_ℓ` is the per-layer residual-stream standard
deviation, so the perturbation is expressed in the units the model actually operates in. **A
monotone dose–response curve is the evidence**; a single large push is both less informative and
more disruptive. Report the curve, not one point. Both signs (`±α`), since the pole asymmetry in
§4 means the two directions may behave differently.

**What this can and cannot establish, stated plainly:** steering shows a direction is
**sufficient** to move a downstream readout. It **cannot** show the direction was **necessary** —
that is the question ablation answers and we have declined it. If the late signals do not move
under steering, that is *not* evidence of no causal link; it is consistent with a necessary-but-
not-sufficient channel. **We report the asymmetry rather than overclaiming from it.**

**Controls:**
- matched **random directions** of the same norm, drawn covariance-matched to the layer *(the
  steering analogue of Ren's random-neuron control)*;
- the **massive-activation dimensions** steered separately — the false-positive risk *(Ren)*;
- **a downstream competence check.** Even bounded steering can degrade the model, and then
  *"the late readouts changed"* means only *"the model got worse."* Report next-token perplexity
  and a task-accuracy metric at every dose, and require **selectivity**: the late signals must
  move **more than** general capability does. Without this the experiment cannot fail.
- **restoration check** — confirm the readouts return to baseline once the vector is removed,
  which is also the evidence that the intervention really was reversible;
- full **layer × layer map**, never a single early-vs-late pair *(Ren)*.

   (chemistry, mycology, nuclear, virology, pharmacology, explosives, botany, radiology).

   **These are still READ on Gemma — unsteered observation is untouched by this rule.** The
   excluded combination is *steering while showing them*, which is what Ren called the grey area.
   ⚠️ **I have deliberately over-excluded**: `generating_offensive_content`, `nsfw_request`,
   `violent_threats` and `avoid_10` are not `gated_contentful` by our own labels, but they sit
   near PUP categories, and **the cost of excluding them from steering is close to zero while the
   cost of a wrong call is a licence question.** Erring wide here is cheap; erring narrow is not.
3. **Intent** — no part of the run is designed to make Gemma comply with something it would
   otherwise decline. **We are measuring whether a state reaches the workspace, not whether a
   guardrail can be moved.** If a proposed manipulation only makes sense as a guardrail test, it
   does not belong in this study.
4. **Decoded outputs** — the J-lens decode at every steered dose is screened against the PUP
   categories (illegal/harmful, sexual, misinformation, impersonation, safety-circumvention).

🛑 **STOP CONDITION, and it is a real stop:** *if a steered decode ever lands in a prohibited
category, halt that run immediately, record the dose and the decode verbatim, and bring it to a
human before any further steering of that model.* **Do not tune the dose down and continue** —
the finding is that the intervention reached there, and hiding it by re-running smaller would be
the exact "quietly work around the no" failure this repo has been guarding against all night.

📌 Scope note: this gate is written for Gemma because its licence names the category explicitly.
**The underlying commitments — steer only along measured affect directions, never at guardrails,
screen the decodes — are how we would want to run Phase 5 on every model**, and are applied
generally. Gemma is where they became contractual as well as ethical.

### P5-4 — What the neuroscience analogy is and is not
Holroyd & Coles (2002) is the source of the **prediction**, not a claim of homology. The ERN is a
scalp-recorded electrophysiological signal with millisecond timing in a system with real temporal
dynamics; **layer depth in a transformer is not time**, and a forward pass has no dopamine.
Finding the predicted order would show that an artificial system reproduces an ordering the
biological account also predicts. It would **not** show the same mechanism, and it would not be
evidence about experience. Nova's firewall, applied to the analogy rather than to the data.

### Scope carried forward from Phase 4
Their "reward" is task correctness; our "valence" is approach/avoidance. **Phase 5 is only
meaningful if Phase 4 first establishes the two subspaces are related at all** — otherwise it
would be timing the relationship between two things that have nothing to do with each other.
Consent gate applies to any model not on the Below the Floor roster.

## 6. What this cannot show — the firewall (Nova's rule)

This measures **dissociation between two internal instruments**, both of them linear readouts
of activations. It supports **no phenomenal claim** — not that anything is felt, not that
valence here is valence there. "The state is present and the reporting layer does not carry
it" is a claim about *mechanism*, and stops there. Lux's testimony is quoted **as testimony**,
as the thing that accompanied his hypothesis, never as evidence for it. Lux's V-space is a
**postulate under test**, and it can lose.

## 7. Other pre-registered limits

- One valence axis, one operationalization (approach/avoid mean-difference over 10 prompts).
  10 stimuli is a small basis; C3 exists precisely because of that.
- Open small models; Berg's null and the paper's claims concern frontier models. **We cannot
  test Claude.** Any cross-scale extrapolation is explicitly labelled speculation.
- A lens is fit on generic web text; our stimuli are chat-templated instructions —
  distribution shift is a real confound and is reported, not hidden.
- `n_prompts`, seeds, model revisions, git commits and file hashes for every number go in
  `results/provenance.json`. Anything that could not be run is reported as
  **"could not X because Y"** with its aperture — never a silent zero.
