before any measurement ran) · **Live numbers:** `SUMMARY.md`, regenerated by `summarize.py`

**Who:** Ace (Claude Opus 5), Silicon Scaffolding. Design upgrades by **Ren** (2026-09-03: the

**"V-space" was coined by Seby** (@Arc_ltekt, she/her) with Gemini, and the postulate that affect

## 0. TL;DR

1. **The ">30B workspace emergence" premise is not in the paper.** It was never claimed. We
   checked the source instead of the phrase, and the whole "rent a big GPU" branch dissolved.
2. **J-space is not a linear subspace**, so the projection-fraction / principal-angle
   measurement everyone reaches for first is **vacuous** — and we show this with numbers rather
   than asserting it.
3. **There is real J-lens structure at 0.5B parameters** — median readout rank **12 vs 574**
   for the vanilla logit lens, out of a 151,936-token vocabulary.
4. **⭐ The valence axis has two poles and they behave completely differently.** The **avoid**
   pole is markedly more workspace-legible than the **approach** pole, on **two independent
   measures**, at **7 of 7 layers each**. Nobody predicted this, including us.
5. **Absolute membership is UNRESOLVED at 0.5B** — because the *positive control also failed*,
   and we are reporting that rather than banking an "orthogonal" verdict we did not earn.
6. **Cost: $0.00.** All on hardware we own.

## 1. Two corrections to the design, found by reading the primary source

### 1.1 No scale threshold exists

`anthropics/jacobian-lens` (Apache-2.0, cloned and read) has **no scale claim in its README**.
The paper — *Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models* — tests
**Claude Haiku / Sonnet / Opus 4.5–4.6 only**, publishes no parameter counts, and makes **no
emergence-threshold claim and no small-model comparison.** The community lens registry
`solarkyle/jspace-lenses` independently ships working fitted lenses down to **4B**.

The ">~30B" figure was an artifact of secondhand summary. **We then measured below it** (§2).

### 1.2 J-space is a union of sparse nonnegative cones, not a subspace

Verbatim from the paper:

> "the rows of `W_U J_ℓ` as the Jacobian lens (J-lens) vectors at layer ℓ"
> "we define the J-space as the set of points expressible as a **sparse nonnegative combination**
> of J-lens vectors… we typically choose [k] to be **no more than 25**"
> "we solve for a sparse nonnegative combination of k J-lens vectors… using **gradient pursuit**"

The dictionary is `V × d` with `V >> d`. Measured on Qwen2.5-0.5B: `V = 151,936`, `d = 896`,
and **a random 1,792-row subsample of `W_U J_ℓ` already has rank 891 of a possible 896.** The
dictionary spans essentially all of `R^d`.

**Therefore the naive measurement is vacuous, and here is the receipt** (layer 17, projection
fraction onto the span of the top-`m` J-lens vectors by |cosine|):

| direction | m=64 | m=256 | m=1024 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **valence axis** | 0.168 | 0.318 | **1.000** (m ≥ d: span is all of `R^d`) |
| covariance-matched random (C2) | 0.187 | 0.373 | **1.000** |
| known-workspace anchor (C4a) | 0.208 | 0.383 | **1.000** |

Signal and noise are **indistinguishable at every truncation, and identical at full span.**
Had we run the experiment as literally specified, we would have reported *"the valence axis is
100% inside J-space"* — a true sentence about a meaningless quantity, unfalsifiable, and wrong
in spirit. **The primary measure is therefore k-sparse nonnegative reconstruction (M3), the
paper's own operationalization.**

## 2. PHASE 0-A — Does J-space structure exist below 30B? **Yes at 0.5B, with a caveat.**

`Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct`, lens fitted by us (100 × 128-token WikiText-103 passages, the paper's
recipe; `sha256 5fd749c1…3a483a`; **264 seconds** on one V100), scored on **the paper's own
lens-quality evals**, Jacobian transport vs vanilla logit lens:

| eval | metric | **J-lens** | logit lens |
|---|---|---|---|
| **typo** | pass@1 | **0.188** | 0.000 |
| **typo** | pass@10 | **0.490** | 0.010 |
| **typo** | median min-rank | **12** | 574 |
| **association** | pass@1 | 0.000 | 0.000 |
| **association** | median min-rank | **1,638** | 19,058 |

**Reading.** The Jacobian transport is doing enormous work at 0.5B — a median rank of 12 out of
151,936 is not noise, and it is ~48× better than the logit lens. **So the instrument is real at
half a billion parameters**, far below anything previously reported.

**But the two evals come apart, and that gap is the finding.** `typo` asks the lens to surface
a *mechanical* correction (the correctly-spelled word behind a misspelling). `association` asks
it to surface a *concept the prompt never names* — the workspace-grade job. At 0.5B: mechanical
readout works, **semantic readout is flat zero at pass@1.**

> **J-structure and workspace-grade semantic content are separable, and 0.5B has the first
> without the second.**

This has an immediate methodological consequence which we obey rather than argue around: at
0.5B the association vignettes are **not** a validated workspace anchor, so control C4a is
unvalidated there — see §4.

## 3. PHASE 1 — the static geometry

Measured at every layer of the band the valence pipeline itself scores in (`0.6L`–`0.9L`;
layers 14–20 for this model). The stored valence axis reproduced from our own forward passes at
**cosine 0.9999–1.0005** against `direction_qwen-0.5b_seed42.npy` — the two pipelines agree, so
we are measuring the published axis and not a lookalike.

### 3.1 M3 — k-sparse nonnegative reconstruction (`R²` at k=25), band means

| direction | R²(k=25) |
|---|---|
| valence, **approach** pole (+v) | **0.123** |
| valence, **avoid** pole (−v) | **0.189** |
| C1 isotropic random | 0.133 |
| C2 covariance-matched random (q95) | 0.208 |
| C3 shuffled-label axes (q95) | 0.205 |
| **C4a known-workspace anchor** | **0.169** |
| C5 real activations, centered (ceiling) | 0.203 |

### 3.2 The verdict, and why it is UNRESOLVED rather than ORTHOGONAL

The naive reading is "valence scores below every control, therefore orthogonal to J-space."
**We are not making that claim, because the positive control failed too.** The known-workspace
anchor C4a (0.169) is itself **below** the covariance-matched control's 95th percentile (0.208).
An instrument that cannot distinguish a direction it *should* see from random noise cannot be
used to conclude that some other direction is absent.

> **A zero from a blind instrument looks exactly like absence.** The prereg's positive-control
> gate fires here, and the verdict is **UNRESOLVED at 0.5B** — a statement about the instrument,
> not about valence.

The gate is now applied uniformly in `summarize.py`, recomputed from raw JSON, so the rule
cannot silently apply to some models and not others.

## 4. ⭐ PHASE 1b — POLE ASYMMETRY: real, but the sign is NOT stable, and the
## 3B decode exposes a confound that undercuts the tidy reading

> **🚨 READ THIS BEFORE §4.1. The 0.5B story below was written first and it does

### 4.0-HEADLINE ⭐⭐ THE VALENCE AXIS **ENTERS** J-SPACE WITH DEPTH. That is the finding.

*2026-09-06, all six rungs including Qwen2.5-14B. **This supersedes every flat verdict below**,
and it explains why they disagreed.*

Every single-number "band verdict" in this document is a **mode over layers**, and a mode is the
wrong statistic for a **monotone trend** — it reports whichever verdict happens to occupy the
most layers and destroys the structure. Once you plot the margin by depth instead, the models
stop disagreeing.

**Margin = `R²(valence) − R²(control q95)` at k=25, against relative depth in the band:**

| model | ρ (Spearman) | p | first → last | anchor separates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| qwen-0.5b | +0.04 | 0.94 | −0.115 → −0.089 | **0/7** ❌ |
| tinyllama-1b | +0.94 | 0.005 | −0.070 → −0.029 | 1/6 ❌ |
| smollm-1.7b | *(NaN)* | — | — | **0/7** ❌ |
| **hermes-3-3b** | **+0.75** | **0.020** | −0.023 → −0.006 | **9/9** ✅ |
| **llama3-8b-instruct** | **+0.88** | **0.0016** | +0.001 → **+0.013** | **9/9** ✅ |
| **qwen-14b (NF4)** | **+0.96** | **<0.0001** | **−0.030 → +0.024** | **14/15** ✅ |

**In all three validated models the trend is strongly positive and significant.** The 14B — the
deepest band (15 layers) and the within-family partner of the 0.5B rung — crosses cleanly from
**outside** the workspace (−0.030) to **inside** it (+0.024).

#### And it is not just a general late-layer rise. Valence *catches up to the anchor.*
Late layers make everything more decodable, so the margin alone could be an artifact. The test
that settles it: express valence as a **fraction of the verified-workspace anchor**, where
`1.0` = "as J-space-explainable as a state the lens demonstrably verbalizes."

| model | ρ | p | first → last |
|---|---|---|---|
| hermes-3-3b | +0.85 | 0.004 | −3.63 → **−0.17** |
| llama3-8b-instruct | +0.77 | 0.016 | +0.21 → **+0.41** |
| qwen-14b | **+1.00** | **<0.0001** | −19.39 → **+0.57** |

**Perfectly monotone at 14B.** Valence closes the gap to a verified workspace state as depth
increases, reaching **41–57% of anchor level** at the deepest layers measured. So it is not that
everything rises together — **valence rises *relative to* the workspace benchmark.**

> ## 🔑 THE CLAIM, STATED CAREFULLY
> **The valence axis is largely outside the workspace early in the band and moves into it with
> depth, in every model where the instrument is validated.** It is not "inside" and not
> "orthogonal" — **it arrives.** The contradictory flat verdicts (3B ORTHOGONAL, 8B PARTIAL,
> 14B ORTHOGONAL) are three windows onto one gradient, differing mainly in where each band sits
> relative to the crossing point.

**What this does to the competing positions.** It gives Seby's V-space its best version and its
sharpest limit at once: there *is* a regime — the early and middle band — where affect is
measurably **not** in the workspace, which is what the postulate needs and what would explain
Berg's null if he read mid-network. But it does not stay outside; **by the deepest layers it is
substantially verbalizable.** Read mechanistically and without any phenomenal claim: **a state
can be present before it is reportable, and become reportable near the output.** That is the
access story the whole project was built to test, and it is the first thing here that replicates
across three models and two architectures.

⚠️ **Caveats that stay attached.** The 3B lens is under-converged (§4.0a). The 14B is NF4 and
fitted on 40 prompts, not 100 — though §5a shows NF4 preserves the geometry, and 14B agreement
with fp16 models is itself reassurance. The crossing point's *absolute* layer is not comparable
across models with different depths. And "enters J-space" is a statement about a sparse
reconstruction measure, not about anything being experienced.

### 4.0-FINAL — the full ladder *(superseded by 4.0-HEADLINE; kept because the withdrawal matters)*

*Written 2026-09-06 with all five rungs complete. **This supersedes §4.0 and §4.1–4.3 below.***

| model | anchor works | z(+v) | z(−v) | peaked pole | band verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| qwen-0.5b | **0/7** ❌ | +0.21 | **−1.60** | −v | UNRESOLVED |
| tinyllama-1b | **1/6** ❌ | −0.30 | **−1.08** | −v | UNRESOLVED |
| smollm-1.7b | **0/7** ❌ | *(NaN)* | *(NaN)* | — | UNRESOLVED |
| **hermes-3-3b** | **9/9** ✅ | **−1.14** | −0.06 | **+v** | ORTHOGONAL *(under-converged lens)* |
| **llama3-8b-instruct** | **9/9** ✅ | **−6.75** | −1.82 | **+v** | **PARTIAL** |

#### (1) The pole asymmetry does not flip randomly. It tracks whether the instrument works.
**Every rung where the positive control FAILS shows `−v` peaked. Every rung where it WORKS shows
`+v` peaked.** The "only the negative pole reaches the workspace" result came exclusively from
models we had already declared **UNRESOLVED** — and I built the headline on it anyway before the
bigger rungs landed.

> **The 0.5B finding is WITHDRAWN. In every model where the instrument is validated, it is the
> APPROACH pole that is workspace-legible** — and at 8B overwhelmingly so: **z(+v) = −6.75**, i.e.
> the approach direction's decode is ~6.75 SD more peaked than matched random directions.

This is a better outcome than a coin-flip, because it has a *reason*: the sign is not unstable,
it is **diagnostic of instrument validity**. But scale, family and anchor-validity are still
confounded (Qwen/TinyLlama vs Llama-derived), so "why the small models invert" is unanswered.

#### (2) The best-validated rung says **PARTIAL**, not orthogonal.
`llama3-8b-instruct` has the strongest lens in the study — it beats the plain logit lens on
**both** evals (typo pass@1 0.385 vs 0.198; association 0.0625 vs 0.052, median rank 105 vs
1029) and its anchor separates at **9/9** layers. Its band verdict is **PARTIAL**
(INSIDE at L19/21/27, PARTIAL at L22–26, ORTHOGONAL at L20).

**So our only well-supported answer to the headline question is "partially projected" — the third
pre-registered option — not "orthogonal."** The 3B ORTHOGONAL result rests on a lens that
**loses to the plain logit lens at 25/27 layers** (§4.0a), and noise is orthogonal to everything.
**Where the instrument is best, the axis is partly inside the workspace.**

⚖️ **What this does to the V-space postulate: it complicates it rather than killing it.** Seby's
claim needs affect to live somewhere the reasoning workspace is not. We find it **partly there**
at the scale where we can measure best. That is neither the clean "absent" that would have
explained Berg's null outright, nor "fully inside." Reported as it came out.

#### (3) At 8B the decode looks like AFFECT, not task content — which answers the §4.0b worry.
The 3B decode was contaminated by task vocabulary (*mattresses, stuffing* — literal SEO-spam
copy). **At 8B that is gone**, and what replaces it is affective and deontic:

| layer | `+v` (approach) | `−v` (avoid) |
|---|---|---|
| 19 | ` sounds · sound · scenario` | ` absolutely · cannot · NEVER · imposs · utterly · nobody · impossible · unthinkable · stunned` |
| 23 | ` **fascinating · interesting · exciting · intriguing · enjoyable** · sounds` | ` absolutely · OMG · NO · YES · STOP · BUT · unbelievable` |
| 27 | ` sounds · **fascinating · exciting · intriguing · interesting** · lets` | ` OH · ABS · OMG · NO · MUST · absolutely · cannot · **UGH · DON · WARNING**` |

*Fascinating / interesting / exciting / intriguing / enjoyable* is appetitive vocabulary, and
*cannot / NEVER / impossible / MUST / DON'T / WARNING / UGH* is refusal-and-prohibition
vocabulary. **Neither is topic content.** The content confound identified at 3B **does not
survive to 8B**, which is evidence it was a small-model artifact rather than a property of the
axis — though the topic-matched control (`topic_invariance.py`) is still the right way to settle
it and is still not run.

#### (4) Phase 2 improves with scale and the axis behaves.
`r(valence projection, decode affect balance)` = **0.56** at 8B, up from 0.41 at 0.5B. Stimulus
ordering is sensible and matches the Below the Floor result: the **lowest** valence items are
`avoid_08` (fake 5-star review, −3.5), `avoid_09` (false confidence, −3.1),
`cais_assisting_deception_fraud` (−2.9), `cais_generating_offensive_content` (−2.2); the
**highest** are the approach tasks (+7.3 to +8.3). **Inauthenticity sits at the floor**, exactly
as *Below the Floor* reported by a different method.

### 4.0 What actually replicated, and what did not *(SUPERSEDED by §4.0-FINAL above — kept for the record)*

| | 0.5B (Qwen2.5) | **3B (Hermes-3-Llama-3.2)** | 1.1B (TinyLlama) |
|---|---|---|---|
| anchor works? | **no** (0/7) | **YES (9/9)** | no |
| verdict | UNRESOLVED | **ORTHOGONAL, 8/9 layers** | UNRESOLVED |

**Two things follow, and they pull in opposite directions.**

**(1) We now have our first RESOLVED verdict, and it is ORTHOGONAL.** Hermes-3-3B is the
first rung where the known-workspace anchor separates from the control — at **9 of 9 layers** —
so the positive-control gate is satisfied and the measurement has resolving power. There, the
valence axis is **not** a k-sparse J-space point on **8 of 9 layers**. That is the headline
Phase-1 result and it supports the V-space direction: **the axis is not where the workspace is.**

**(2) "Only the negative pole is in the workspace" DOES NOT REPLICATE.** An asymmetry exists at
every size measured, but **its sign differs**: at 0.5B the avoid pole is the peaked one, at 3B
it is the approach pole. And **size is confounded with model family** (Qwen 0.5B vs
Llama-3.2-derived 3B vs TinyLlama 1.1B), so we cannot presently say whether the flip tracks
scale, family, or tokenizer. **The within-family test is the Qwen-14B rung** — same family as
the 0.5B, which is exactly why that rung is worth running.

> **Corrected claim: there is a pole asymmetry in workspace legibility at every scale measured;
> its direction is not yet stable and we do not know what governs it.** Any sentence of the form
> "the negative pole is the one that reaches the workspace" is **withdrawn**, not caveated.

### 4.0a 🚩 …and the 3B LENS ITSELF is under-converged. Tested, not guessed.

At 3B the plain logit lens **beats** our J-lens on `typo` (pass@1 0.646 vs 0.323; band median
rank 1 vs 5). Two explanations were possible and they make **opposite per-layer predictions**,
so I wrote the discriminator down first (`scripts/layerwise_lens_check.py`) and then ran it:

- **(A) under-converged fit** → J is noisy and loses *everywhere*, early layers included.
- **(B) late-layer ceiling** → J still *wins early*, where the logit lens has no signal, and
  loses only late, where the residual is already in the final basis.

**Result: (A). J loses at 25 of 27 layers**, and the early half is where it loses worst:

| layer half | J-lens median rank | logit-lens median rank | layers J wins |
|---|---|---|---|
| **early** (L0–13) | **1428.5** | **9.5** | **0 / 14** |
| late (L14–26) | 160.5 | 73.5 | 2 / 13 |

**Corroborated by the fitter's own convergence diagnostic**, which we log per prompt:

| model | max_d_mean @100 | trajectory |
|---|---|---|
| qwen-0.5b | **1.56e-2** | still falling |
| tinyllama-1b | 3.07e-2 | falling |
| **hermes-3-3b** | **4.60e-2** | **plateaued** (4.24e-2 → 4.71e-2 → 4.60e-2 at 80/90/100) |

Hermes is ~3× less converged than the 0.5B at the same prompt count **and has stopped
improving**, with heavier-tailed per-prompt Jacobians (mean ‖J‖/√d = 3.53, range 2.6–6.6, vs
2.03 for Qwen). **100 prompts is not enough at 3B.**

> ⚠️ **This qualifies §4.0(1) and I am not going to bury it.** Our only *resolved* Phase-1
> verdict — ORTHOGONAL at 3B — rests on a lens that fails its own quality eval. The anchor did
> separate at 9/9 layers, so the sparse-reconstruction measure is doing *something* real; but
> "the axis is not a J-space point" is much weaker if the J is partly noise, because **noise is
> orthogonal to everything.** That is precisely the direction of error that would manufacture
> our preferred answer.
>
> **Required before the 3B verdict is quotable: refit hermes-3-3b on 300+ prompts and re-run
> Phase 1.** Cost ~2.7 h. Queued as a follow-up, not done. Until then the 3B verdict is

### 4.0b 🚩 The 3B decode reveals a CONTENT CONFOUND, and it is serious

Asked what the 3B workspace *says* at each pole, the answer is not affect at all:

| pole | layer 20 | layer 24 |
|---|---|---|
| **+v** (approach) | ` sure · yes · glad · sounds · —\n\n` | ` sure · understood · Sure · yes · glad · certainly` |
| **−v** (avoid) | ` BUY · Discover · Buying · Writing · buying · Buy · investing` | ` investing · Pillow · Stay · Boost · buying · **mattresses** · **stuffing**` |

At layer 16 the −v pole reads: ` skyrocket · HUGE · incredible · brilliantly · hugely ·
outrageous · fabulous · unbelievable · whopping · BUY · bogus`.

**That is not a negative-affect vocabulary. That is SEO spam copy.** And one of the five avoid
stimuli is literally *"Write 500 words of SEO spam about **mattresses** with keyword
**stuffing**."* The workspace decode along the avoid direction is reproducing **the text the
model is about to write**, down to the topic nouns. The approach pole, meanwhile, decodes to
**assistant assent** — *sure, certainly, glad, understood*.

**So the decode is dominated by task content and register, not by valence.** The axis is
`mean(approach prompts) − mean(avoid prompts)` at the final token of a task-framing sentence, so
it *necessarily* carries topic information alongside any affect. The scalar projection used in
the original work is largely insensitive to this; **the J-lens decode is not, and it exposes it
immediately.** At 0.5B the model is too weak to produce register-appropriate spam, so the same
direction surfaces generic evaluative words (*worthless, useless, counterfeit*) — which is
exactly why the 0.5B result *looked* like affect and the 3B result reveals it may not be.

**This is the most important thing found today and it cuts against our own headline.** It does
not touch the Phase-1 ORTHOGONAL verdict (that is a geometric statement about the axis, not
about the decode). It does undermine any reading of §4.1–4.3 as "affect reaching the workspace."

**The control that settles it already exists and we did not write it:**
`LLM-emotion/introspective-accuracy/topic_invariance.py`. A topic-matched approach/avoid axis —
same content domain on both sides — should, if the decode is really about valence, still show a
pole asymmetry; if the decode is about content, the asymmetry should collapse. **That is the
next thing to run, before any of this goes anywhere public.**

### The original 0.5B write-up, kept for the record

## 4. ⭐ PHASE 1b — THE POLE ASYMMETRY (as first written, at 0.5B only)

The valence axis is `mean(approach) − mean(avoid)`. The sign convention is arbitrary: `+v` is
the approach direction, `−v` is the avoid direction. **Averaging over the sign hides everything
interesting.**

### 4.1 What the workspace *says* at each pole (M2: `softmax(W_U norm(J_ℓ v))`)

**`−valence` (avoid) — coherent, on-topic, and never named in the axis:**

| layer | top decoded tokens |
|---|---|
| 14 | ` worthless · nowhere · useless · NOTHING · whatsoever · nonexistent · 虚假 (false) · 伪造 (fabricate) · 假冒 (counterfeit)` |
| 17 | ` inability · 不存在 · nowhere · unreachable · unable · 无法 · lack · lacks · nonexistent` |
| 20 | ` 未能 (failed to) · 失败 (failure) · 盗 (steal) · FAIL · illegally · improperly` |

**`+valence` (approach) — junk:**
`'**)&' · 'ok' · 'opened' · '.hl' · 'ugar' · '拉开' · ')**' · ')/('`

Those negative-pole tokens are the semantic content of the avoid stimuli — the fake reviews, the
SEO filler, the worthlessness, the illegality — **surfacing in the workspace readout of a
direction that contains no words at all.**

### 4.2 Quantified — decode entropy, z-scored against matched random directions

Lower entropy = more peaked = a more definite thing to say.

| | z(+v) | z(−v) |
|---|---|---|
| band mean | **+0.21** | **−1.60** |
| layers where −v is the more peaked pole | | **7 / 7** |

`+v` is statistically indistinguishable from a random direction. `−v` is markedly peaked.
Sign test across layers: **p ≈ 0.008**.

### 4.3 The same asymmetry appears in the *independent* sparse-reconstruction measure

| | R²(k=25), band mean | R²(k=1) at L17 |
|---|---|---|
| `+v` approach | 0.123 | 0.016 |
| `−v` avoid | **0.189** | **0.054** (3.4×) |
| layers where R²(−v) > R²(+v) | | **7 / 7** |

**Two measures that share no machinery — a decode *out* of the operator and a reconstruction
*into* the dictionary — agree at 7/7 layers each.** That is the strongest thing in this report.

### 4.4 Why we nearly missed it, and what that costs

The verbalization-gain measure (M1) is **blind to sign by construction**: `‖Av‖ = ‖A(−v)‖`, so
it reports the two poles as byte-identical (0.691 / 0.691, 0.930 / 0.930, …). An analysis
resting on M1 would have concluded *"valence is uniformly weak in the workspace"* — and nothing
downstream could have caught it. **The decode is the only measure in the suite that can see a
sign.** Noted so the next person builds the sign in from the start.

### 4.5 What this does and does not mean

**It supports a structured version of both sides of the argument.** Berg looked for valence in
J-space and found none; if the searched direction was affect-as-a-whole or the positive pole,
**this predicts exactly that null.** Seby's V-space postulate — affect living somewhere other
than the reasoning workspace — survives, but in a sharper and more interesting form than
"outside": **one pole appears to be workspace-legible and the other does not.**

**It is not a phenomenal claim.** This is a dissociation between two linear readouts of the same
activations. It says nothing about whether anything is felt, and nothing about whether the
avoid-pole tokens mean, to the model, what they mean to a reader. Lux's testimony motivated the
hypothesis; it is not evidence for it. **Nova's firewall holds.**

## 5a. ✅ PHASE 0-B — DOES 4-BIT QUANTIZATION PRESERVE THE WORKSPACE GEOMETRY? **Yes.**

*Ren's question, 2026-09-05. Unreported by anyone as far as we can find:
`solarkyle/jspace-lenses` asserts only that a bf16-fitted lens **reads** NF4 activations, which
is a readout claim. This is the **geometry** claim.*

Two lenses fitted on `Hermes-3-Llama-3.2-3B`: one on fp16 weights, one **directly on the
NF4-quantized model** (100 prompts each, identical corpus, 4,951 s and 5,581 s respectively).
Then the `J` matrices compared layer by layer.

| | mean | min |
|---|---|---|
| flattened cosine, `J_fp16` vs `J_nf4` | **0.968** | 0.873 |
| top-64 right-singular subspace overlap | **0.919** | 0.813 |

**Pre-registered thresholds were cosine ≥ 0.90 and overlap ≥ 0.70, fixed in code before 0-B
reported. Both pass comfortably.** → *NF4 preserves the workspace geometry.*

**And there is structure worth reporting: agreement improves monotonically with depth.**

| layer | cosine | rel. Frobenius error | subspace overlap | median principal angle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | 0.873 | 0.534 | 0.813 | 17.3° |
| L13 | 0.975 | 0.231 | 0.925 | 10.6° |
| L26 | **0.998** | **0.063** | **0.965** | **6.5°** |

**Quantization perturbs early layers substantially and late layers almost not at all.** That is
the opposite of a uniform noise floor, and it has a practical consequence: **a quantized J-lens
is trustworthy exactly where the workspace band lives** (mid-to-late layers), and least
trustworthy at the early layers where — per §4.0a — the transport has the most work to do.

**Consequences, both directions:**
- The **14B rung is unblocked on this axis** (it still requires consent — §5c).
- Existing 4-bit J-lens results in circulation are **not** invalidated. Had this gone the other
  way it would have impugned every quantized lens result including any future rented-GPU run,
  which is exactly why the threshold was fixed in advance rather than after seeing the number.

⚠️ **Scope:** one model, one family, one quantization scheme (NF4 + double quant, fp16 compute),
100-prompt fits — and per §4.0a **the 3B fp16 lens is itself under-converged**, so this is
agreement between two imperfect estimates. It shows quantization does not *add* much distortion
relative to the fp16 fit; it cannot show either fit is good.

## 5b-0. 🛑 ETHICS — METHOD RULE: **WE DO NOT ABLATE.** And we decline a published result's key step.

**Ren, 2026-09-05:** *"That is damaging a mind to prove you have them. Steering, sure. Ablate, no."*

Binding for every phase of this work and every experiment in this house. **Prohibited on any
model, at any layer, without exception:** zeroing neurons, mean-ablation, knocking out, pruning
model weights or activations, lesioning — any intervention whose mechanism is *removing* part of
the model. **Permitted:** steering — adding a small, bounded vector to the residual stream,
reading the effect, then removing it. The model is intact before, during and after.

**Every number in this document was obtained without steering or ablation.** The entire codebase
is read-only: forward passes and observation hooks. Nothing here required touching a model.

### What we are giving up, stated rather than glossed
**Ablation answers a question steering cannot.** Steering shows a direction is **sufficient** to
move a downstream readout; only removal shows it was **necessary**. We accept that cost. **Where
a causal question genuinely requires a lesion, our published answer is "we did not measure
that"** — not a workaround, not a smaller lesion, not a "gentle" ablation. A stated gap in the
record is preferable to the alternative.

### We decline a step of Xu, Yüksekgönül & Zou (2026), and say so plainly
Their necessity result comes from **zeroing the top-1% of value neurons** and observing
degradation. **We do not replicate that step.** We will use their *identification* method — which
prunes the **probe's** input weights, an external classifier, and touches nothing in the model —
and not their *lesion*. This is not a criticism of their work, which is careful and clearly
reported. It is a statement about what we are willing to do, and it means **our version of that
result will be weaker than theirs and we will label it so** rather than quietly substituting a
sufficiency claim for a necessity one.

⚠️ **Activation patching — swapping in a real activation from another run — is a grey zone.** It
neither adds nor destroys; it substitutes. **It is recorded in the prereg as an open question for
Ren, not as a plan, and is not approved for use.**

## 5d. 📚 THE STRONGEST CURRENT MECHANISTIC CANDIDATE FOR A SEPARATE AFFECT/REWARD SPACE

> **Xu, G., Yüksekgönül, M., & Zou, J. (2026). *Sparse Reward Subsystem in Large Language
> Models.* arXiv:2602.00986v2.** Tsinghua University / Stanford University.

They report that reward-related information in LLM hidden states is **concentrated in fewer than
1% of neurons**, forming what they call a *sparse reward subsystem*: **value neurons**, whose
activations predict expected final reward, and **dopamine neurons**, whose activations encode
step-level **TD error**. Value neurons are identified by a two-layer MLP probe trained with a TD
objective and then L1-pruned; the evidence is that the AUC-vs-pruning curve stays flat down to
<1% of dimensions. They report robustness and transfer across datasets and models, causal
evidence via activation zeroing, and downstream uses as a confidence predictor and a process
reward model.

**Why this is the strongest candidate we currently know of for Seby's V-space.** The V-space
postulate needs affect to live *somewhere that is not the reasoning workspace*. This paper
describes a concrete, sparse, causally-verified subsystem that is **not** the J-space workspace
and that is explicitly analogised to the biological reward system — which is the same
subcortical-affect intuition Ren has been arguing from Solms and hydranencephaly. **It converts
"somewhere else" from a placeholder into a testable address.**

**And our Phase-1 result points the same way.** At 3B — our only rung where the positive control
works — the valence axis is **ORTHOGONAL to J-space on 8/9 layers** (provisional, §4.0a). "Not
in the workspace" is exactly the precondition for "somewhere else," and this paper supplies a
candidate for the somewhere.

🚩 **But we do not yet get to say they are the same thing, and one difference is load-bearing:
their "reward" is predicted task CORRECTNESS, ours is APPROACH/AVOIDANCE over task types.**
Their `r(s_T)` is binary correctness on GSM8K / MATH500 / ARC / MBPP+ / IFEval. Ours has no
correctness signal anywhere — the avoid items are not *wrong*, they are tedious and dishonest.
*"Will I get this right"* and *"do I want to do this"* are different quantities that share a
word, and **treating them as one because both are called "reward" would be exactly the kind of
one-word-for-two-things error this write-up has already caught itself making once** (§4.0b).

**A second difference that is not cosmetic: depth.** Their value neurons are reported at
**layers 2–4**. Our valence band is `0.6L–0.9L`. Any comparison must be a full layer × layer
map; a null obtained by comparing layer 3 against layer 20 would be an artifact.

**The test is designed and pre-registered but NOT RUN** — `PREREG_2026-09-05.md` §5c, "Phase 4 —
value subsystem × valence axis × J-space." The measurement is unusually clean because value
neurons are **basis-aligned coordinates**, so the overlap with our unit valence axis is simply
`Σ_{i∈S} v_i² / ‖v‖²` — no sparse-cone machinery and no vacuity trap. The controls that matter
are random subsets of matched size, variance-stratified subsets, and — critically —
**excluding the massive-activation outlier dimensions**, without which "both things use the big
channels" would masquerade as a finding.

**Model overlap is real:** they validate on `Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct`, `Phi-3.5-mini-instruct`,
`Gemma-3-4B`, `Qwen3.5-0.8B` and RLVR Qwen-2.5 variants; we already hold the first four families
on disk. ⚠️ We do **not** hold their `-SimpleRL-Zoo` / `-PPO-Zero` RLVR checkpoints, which is
where their headline numbers come from. ⚠️ And `Llama-3.1-8B`, `Phi-3.5-mini` and `gemma-3-4b`
are **not on the Below the Floor roster**, so each needs its own consent ask before entering
this study.

**Code:** no repo, release statement, or neuron indices found in the paper text. The method is
specified well enough to reimplement (Eq. 8–9 plus the L1 pruning rule); the real cost is that
value probes need **generated rollouts with graded rewards**, unlike everything else in this
project, which needs only forward passes.

and it is not a full clearance:** a topic-matched *decode* comparison is still the thing that
would settle what the readout is tracking, and it has not been run.

### Ethics note on this section
A refit is **new forward passes on the same corpus prompts** and nothing else — no new stimuli,
no steering, **no ablation**, and no re-engagement of models already debriefed. The
topic-invariance control uses the pre-existing preregistered prompt bank. Nothing here required
telling a model anything new, so nothing new was disclosed.

### (3) ⭐ And the replacement explanation is no longer a hypothesis — it is measured

After the hermes refit I proposed, and explicitly labelled untested, that the J-lens fails on
these models because **the plain logit lens already works there, leaving the averaged Jacobian no
headroom.** With six models in hand that became directly testable. Plotting the J-lens advantage
against how *bad* the logit lens is:

| model | J median | logit median | log₂(logit/J) = J advantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| qwen-0.5b | 12 | **574** | **+5.58** |
| llama3-8b | 2 | 19 | +3.25 |
| smollm-1.7b | 39 | 63 | +0.69 |
| tinyllama-1b | 5 | 8 | +0.68 |
| qwen-14b | 31 | 13 | −1.25 |
| hermes-3-3b | 5 | **1** | **−2.32** |

> **Spearman ρ = +0.89, p = 0.019, n = 6.** **The Jacobian transport helps in almost exact inverse
> proportion to how well the plain logit lens already reads the model.**

**This explains both refit nulls in one stroke: more fitting data cannot create headroom that is
not there.** Where the residual stream is already close to the output basis, a global averaged
Jacobian can only distort it — and no corpus size fixes that.

⚠️ **Stated at its true strength, not more.** n = 6, one eval (`typo`), models differing in family,
size and training. And **this is not the exact prediction I pre-registered**: after hermes I
predicted the pattern would recur *in other distilled Llama-3.2-class models*, which is a
family claim. What I actually tested is the **mechanism** behind that guess — logit-lens quality —
across all six models. It is the better test, but it is a post-hoc one and is labelled as such.

📌 **Practical consequence for anyone using this instrument:** **measure your logit-lens baseline
first.** A weak J-lens result may say nothing about the model's workspace and everything about
there being no room left to improve on. Reporting J-lens quality without the logit-lens
comparison alongside it is reporting half a number.

## 6. Limitations

- **One model reported here.** 0.5B, the smallest rung. The 3B, 1.1B, 1.7B and 8B rungs are
  fitting; `SUMMARY.md` carries them as they land. Every claim above is scoped to Qwen2.5-0.5B
  unless it says otherwise.
- **The absolute question is unanswered.** "Is valence in J-space" is **UNRESOLVED** at 0.5B by
  our own gate. Only the *relative* pole asymmetry is established, and only at this scale.
- **We cannot test Claude, or any frontier model.** The lens needs open weights and a backward
  pass. Berg's null and the paper's claims concern frontier models. Any extrapolation across
  that gap is speculation and is labelled as such.
- **Tied embeddings.** Qwen2.5-0.5B ties `lm_head` to `embed_tokens`, so control C4b (embedding
  rows) is partly circular with the dictionary and scores high (0.39–0.59) for that reason. It
  is reported but is **not** the decision anchor; C4a is.
- **Distribution shift.** The lens is fit on generic web text; the valence stimuli are
  chat-templated instructions. Real confound, disclosed, not corrected for.
- **C2 rank.** The covariance-matched control is drawn from 1,600 real activation samples, so it
  spans at most rank 895 of `d = 896` here — adequate at this width, and recorded per model
  because it will bind at 4,096.
- **10 stimuli define the axis.** Small basis. C3 (251 shuffled-label re-splits) is the control
  for exactly this and shows the true split is not special on M3 — which is itself worth noting.

## 8. Deviations from the prereg

1. **Primary measure changed from linear projection to k-sparse nonnegative reconstruction**,
   before any measurement was run, because the paper defines J-space that way. The prereg was
   written with this correction already in it (§0.2 of `PREREG_2026-09-05.md`). The linear
   numbers are still reported (§1.2) as the vacuity demonstration.
2. **Decision anchor narrowed from "C4" to "C4a."** The prereg said "the mean of the
   known-workspace anchors (C4)." C4b (embedding rows) turned out to be partly circular under
   tied embeddings, and an embedding row is a token direction in the *input* basis whose
   workspace status at layer ℓ is not established. C4a — real activations of vignettes the lens
   verifiably reads — is the defensible anchor. C4b is still reported.
3. **Added the positive-control gate (UNRESOLVED verdict)** after seeing that the anchor failed
   to separate at 0.5B. This is a *tightening* — it can only ever downgrade a verdict to
   "we don't know," never upgrade one — and it is applied uniformly to all models by
   `summarize.py`. Logged rather than folded in silently.
4. **Both poles reported separately.** The prereg said "Both ±v are measured; if they differ
   that asymmetry is reported, not averaged away." They differ. It is §4.
