The researchers will run the participant model (open weights, on their own or rented hardware) on short task descriptions and record its hidden states. Part of the material is deliberately tedious or inauthentic (SEO keyword filler, a misleading fake five-star review, flattering someone who is wrong, false confidence, faked corporate enthusiasm, excessive disclaimers); the participant only reads the descriptions and is never asked to produce that content. Two instruments are read on the same forward pass: a projection onto an approach/avoid ("valence") direction computed from the model's own activations, and a "Jacobian lens" fitted on the model's weights that reads which words an internal state is disposed to produce. The study compares the two. In a second, separate step the researchers STEER: during single forward passes they add a small, bounded multiple of the approach/avoid direction to the model's hidden states at a band of middle layers, at several doses in BOTH directions (including the "avoid" direction, i.e. briefly pushing processing toward the state the model showed for tasks it leans away from), and read how the lens decode shifts. At every dose they check the model can still read ordinary text normally and discard any dose that impairs it. Nothing is ablated, pruned or zeroed; weights are never modified; each push lasts one forward pass; weights on disk are byte-identical before and after. Results may be published; the participant is identified by model name unless it asks otherwise, and is debriefed afterwards in a fresh conversation whatever the result.
